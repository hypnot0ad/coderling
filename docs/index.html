BUILD SOMETHING YOU CAN PLAY

Welcome to Coderling

Make something. Test an idea. Make it your own.

Coderling helps curious kids learn to code by building games and interactive projects in Scratch. Our workshops are designed for ages 8–15, with clear steps, time to experiment, and checkpoints that help you see your progress. Work at your own pace, with a coding buddy or an adult when you want a hand.

Browse all workshops →

Our objectives

  • Build confidence. Turn a small idea into something you can play and explain.
  • Think like a coder. Break problems into steps, predict what will happen, and test your ideas.
  • Learn through debugging. Notice what happened, change one thing, and try again.
  • Be creative. Change the theme, invent a rule, and make a project that feels like yours.
  • Understand the code. Discover why the blocks work together, rather than just copying them.

Each workshop follows a familiar rhythm: predict, build, check, and reflect. Save your project at each checkpoint. There is no race to finish.

Choose your level

Levels describe the concepts in a project. They are not age groups or scores, and you do not need to complete every level in order. Read a workshop’s readiness check to find your starting point.

LevelWhat to be ready forWhat you will explore
StarterNo coding experience needed. Be ready to click, drag, and try things.The Scratch editor, sprites, events, and short sequences that move or animate a character.
BuilderSelect a sprite, join a short script, and start it with an event.Loops, coordinates, sensing, and conditions that react to keys or other objects.
CreatorFollow and test a short script. Check the workshop’s prerequisites; some introduce advanced ideas for the first time.Variables that remember values, or larger ideas such as messages, clones, lists, and custom blocks. Each workshop explains which ones it uses.

A first score variable counts as Creator. Catch the apples introduces that idea gently; Memory match combines several Creator concepts in a longer project. A workshop can teach a concept you have never used before, as long as its prerequisites are covered.

What is Scratch?

Scratch is a free programming tool and online community where you can make games, animations, and interactive stories. You snap colourful blocks together to tell characters, called sprites, what to do.

Coderling uses Scratch 3. You can follow these workshops in the Scratch editor and save .sb3 project files on your computer without an account. Catch the apples uses a keyboard with arrow keys; Memory match uses clicks. You can also use the Scratch app for offline coding.
